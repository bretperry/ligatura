# Recall, not judgment, limits allusion finding

The 2023–2026 literature agrees on one thing ligatura's own numbers already hint at: **verbatim reuse is close to solved, loose allusion is not, and the bottleneck is getting the right pair into the candidate list, not judging it once it is there.** On the strongest new Latin benchmark, lemma-level BM25 beats every dense bi-encoder at allusion recall (R@100 29.4 vs 20.6 for fine-tuned bge-m3), and the only large gain came from a cross-encoder *fine-tuned on in-domain pairs*, not an off-the-shelf reranker ([Loci Similes](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07533v3)). In the most complete LLM pipeline published, about 94% of missed allusions never reached the judge, and no audited correct pair was rejected once shown ([Cameron 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19447v1)). ligatura's Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B result (Kempis R@1 32 → 29–30; Latin R@1 256 → 210) matches the reasoning-retrieval literature, where most rerankers subtract from a good first stage ([ReasonRank](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07050)). The highest-value changes are therefore cheap first-stage signals the literature validates (rare-word *pairs*, lemmas, neighbourhood alignment, token-level matching, cheap in-domain adaptation), a reranker that is fused rather than substituted, and harder gold sets: Kempis→Gospels (92% R@10) is easier than any allusion benchmark in the literature and too small (49 links, so one link is 2 points) to steer decisions alone. All model training and every full eval below belong on the Mac mini (`ssh riverofsouls`), not the MacBook.

## Every benchmark shows the same verbatim-to-allusion cliff

Papers that split results by link type find one pattern. On Loci Similes (Latin, Jerome and Lactantius against ten classical authors), **BM25 over CLTK lemmas reaches R@100 77.6 on verbatim links but 29.4 on allusions**; fine-tuned bge-m3 gets 63.5 / 20.6 and E5-large 63.1 / 18.3. Allusion *classification* F1 sits near 0.11–0.13 for every transformer, against 0.78 for verbatim ([Loci Similes v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07533v3)). The authors give three reasons for the lexical lead: lemmas handle Latin morphology better than subword tokens, mean-pooled embeddings hide short local cues, and allusions still share about 1.65 lemmas on average. Untuned LatinBERT *token* embeddings (R@10 0.485 pooled) beat every fine-tuned pooled bi-encoder, which points at pooling, not model quality, as the weakness.

The cliff repeats elsewhere. A Biblical Hebrew SBERT fine-tune reaches **R@10 87.1% on narrative synoptic parallels and under 9% on poetic parallels** ([MiqraBERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19638)). A non-neural NT Psalms pipeline scores 0.98 on literal quotations and 0.38 on implicit echoes ([Religions 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel17010088)). The McCarthy→KJV study recovers 80% of "register collisions" but 30% of transformed imagery ([Cameron 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19447v1)). Rabbinic quotation detection reaches F1 0.91 with morphology-aware alignment, against 0.78 for Dicta and 0.62 for passim ([ACT](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23504v1)).

ligatura's measured numbers sit on the easy side of this cliff. Kempis→Gospels at **R@10 45/49 (92%)** and `hbo-parallels` at 98.5% are higher than every allusion-level figure above, which says those gold sets lean toward quotation and close paraphrase. The real headroom is in the cross-lingual fixtures: `augustine-drc` (la→en) finds 203 of 712 links in the top 10 (29%), `saao-akk-en` 141 of 1,944 (7%), and the README already records that Latin→English has only 71.9% of its links in the top 1,000 (README, measured 2026-11-09 to 2026-11-14). No reranker can lift those numbers, because the links are not in the candidate list.

Gold is also incomplete everywhere. Printed edition footnotes caught **only 48% of midrash quotations** that experts found, at precision 1.00 ([ACT](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23504v1)), and the Loci Similes authors say some of their "false positives" are likely real links. Kempis and Augustine are scored against edition footnotes and sidenotes, so ligatura's precision and R@1 are lower bounds, and some "misses" at R@1 may be the model preferring an equally valid verse.

## Signals the literature validates that ligatura lacks

**Lemmas.** Loci Similes' best retriever is lemma BM25, and older allusion work found lemmatized input improved nearly every model ([Manjavacas et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.02973)). ligatura's README lists suffix stripping as a known limit for Latin and Greek and notes Sanskrit has no sandhi splitter. Neural tools exist: ByT5-Sanskrit does sandhi segmentation and lemmatization in one model ([Nehrdich et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.805)), and Coptic and Egyptian lemmatizers were published in 2024 ([Sahala & Lincke](https://aclanthology.org/2024.ml4al-1.10/)). For a JS tool, the practical route is a precomputed form→lemma lookup exported from treebanks, which keeps runtime cost at zero.

**Rare-word pairs, not single rare words.** Tesserae's core rule, two or more shared lemmas weighted by rarity and by the distance between the two rarest matches ([Tesserae scoring](https://tesserae.caset.buffalo.edu/blog/?p=186)), reportedly captures about 67% of commentary-noted intertexts ([DH abstract](https://dh-abstracts.library.virginia.edu/works/2473)). Its team later proposed PMI pair scoring because rarity alone underrates Vergil's unusual combinations of common words ([Tesserae blog](https://tesserae.caset.buffalo.edu/blog/?p=1241)). Loci Similes names "two-word congruencies" as the class dense models miss. ligatura scores single rare words, stems and char n-grams; a best-pair score is a new list, not a tweak.

**Token-level matching.** The pooling diagnosis above, plus BRIGHT results where 32–150M ColBERT models match 7–8B dense embedders (Reason-ModernColBERT 22.6 vs Qwen3-Embedding-8B 22.5 nDCG@10) ([SmallReason-ColBERT](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29652v1)), both favour late interaction. bge-m3 already has a multi-vector head; on MIRACL it scores 69.0 vs 67.8 for dense, and all three heads 70.0 ([BGE-M3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03216); figures via a [secondary summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/bge-m3-embedding)). Whether transformers.js exposes bge-m3's `colbert_linear` output is unverified.

**Neighbourhood alignment.** Passim, BLAST-based tools and Smith-Waterman variants all extend seed matches along both texts ([passim](https://github.com/dasmiq/passim); [textreuse](https://packages.ropensci.org/textreuse/textreuse.pdf); [Vierthaler & Gelein](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/a-blast-based-language-agnostic-text-reuse-algorithm-with-a-marku/)). This is proven for verbatim reuse and untested for loose allusion. ligatura's `--neighbours 1` only loosens *scoring*; it does not let a strong link at (n, x) raise (n+1, x+1) in *ranking*.

**Cheap in-domain adaptation.** A 2026 patristics paper reports that SimCSE-style contrastive adaptation (CSE) of a specialist encoder on raw in-domain sentences beat multilingual, distilled and *supervised fine-tuned* baselines on Bible-reuse retrieval in Augustine, Jerome and Athanasius, peaking at **about 4–8k raw sentences and training in tens of seconds on a laptop GPU** ([de la Selle et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24542)). Exact numbers were not visible in the abstract. This is label-free and directly matches `augustine-vulgate`.

**Instructions, for models trained to use them.** Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (MMTEB 64.33, ONNX in transformers.js ≥3.6, Apache-2.0) loses "approximately 1% to 5%" without a query instruction ([model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B)); Promptriever showed non-instruction-trained retrievers get *worse* when given one ([Weller et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/2cefdb2c4c3274b78cd450bac35228df-Abstract-Conference.html)). nomic and bge-m3 will not benefit. No study tests allusion-style instructions.

## Off-the-shelf rerankers subtract; fused or fine-tuned ones help

On BRIGHT, reranking a strong first stage (ReasonIR-8B, 30.59 nDCG@10) usually hurts: RankZephyr-7B 22.64, Rank-R1-7B 20.57, Rank1-7B 27.23, RankT5-3B 16.60. Only listwise reasoners of 7B and up improve it (ReasonRank-7B 35.74, -32B 38.03) ([ReasonRank](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07050)). Pointwise chain-of-thought makes things worse at small sizes: a 1.5B Qwen2.5 reranker drops from 19.0 to 15.6 on BRIGHT when it reasons, because reasoning pushes scores to 0 or 1 and destroys ordering among near-misses ([Jedidi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16886)). Off-the-shelf cross-encoders also lowered nDCG versus the dense first stage on patents ([arXiv 2605.24297](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24297v1)). ligatura's two measured reranker runs (Kempis R@1 32 → 29–30; Latin R@1 256 → 210 at 58 minutes) are this same failure.

Two remedies have evidence. First, **fuse instead of replace**: interpolation with first-stage scores helps zero-shot LLM rerankers and hurts in-domain-trained ones ([Zhuang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13243), from a search snippet, pre-window), and JudgeRank found ensembles of judges beat any one ([arXiv 2411.00142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00142)). Second, **fine-tune on in-domain pairs**: Loci Similes' BM25 → fine-tuned XLM-R Large reranker roughly doubles F1 at k=5 (0.19 → 0.42 in v3) using 1:1 to 1:10 positive:negative ratios, and its negative-sampling ablation shows mixed hard + query-conditioned random negatives yield 7,084 false positives against 17,554 for hard-only ([Loci Similes v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07533v3)). Size matters more than precision: Qwen3-Reranker-4B scores 72.7 MMTEB-R vs 66.4 for 0.6B ([Qwen3 Embedding](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05176)), and Rank1's int4 quantization cost 7B and 14B models 4–6 points but not 32B ([Rank1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18418)), which suggests quantizing a larger model beats running a 0.6B at fp32. Nobody has published a 1–8B open model judging allusion, so any local-LLM judge is an experiment, not a known win.

For a judge, the literature also prescribes the shape. Setwise or listwise comparison ("which of these candidates does this passage echo, if any?") keeps most of pairwise quality at far lower cost ([Zhuang et al. SIGIR 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09497v1.pdf)). LLMs over-attribute: 90% of expert-prompted Woolf–Homer analyses claimed classical transformations ([Werner & Reiter](https://anthology.ach.org/volumes/vol0003/between-woolf-homer-explorative-approach-to-using/)), and LLMs struggle with long query passages ([Umphrey et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01882)). Keep units short, allow "none", and avoid naming the source tradition in the prompt.

## New modalities and gold sets worth testing

Evidence for modalities beyond words and embeddings is uneven. **Strong**: rare-pair co-occurrence and neighbourhood alignment (above). **Moderate, from general IR, not allusion**: LLM-written views per unit (theme, image/motif, claim, register), each embedded and fused as a separate list; Doc2Query++ found separate indices for original and generated text limit expansion noise ([arXiv 2510.09557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09557)), and proposition-level units beat passages in dense retrieval ([Dense X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06648)). A "claim" view is the only plausible handle on philosophical extension, where vocabulary and stance can both change; use stance to label links, never to filter, since refutations are allusions too. Reasoning-style LLM query expansion lifted BRIGHT BM25 from 17.0 to 34.9 with a 14B model ([ThinkQE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09260)), and a source-blind "register" reading track found McCarthy allusions with no shared vocabulary ([Cameron 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19447v1)), but neither has been shown with a small local model. **Weak**: sound (Tesserae's character-trigram search and its *resonante* / *tunditur unda* example, with no recall figures; [Tesserae](https://tesserae.caset.buffalo.edu/blog/?p=360)), meter, syntax templates (one 2008 paper; [Bamman & Crane](https://static.perseus.tufts.edu/publications/latech2008.pdf)), metaphor detection and AMR. For cross-lingual pairs, LaBSE was best of LASER and LaBSE on Greek and Latin alignment ([Craig et al. CHR 2023](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3558/paper6193.pdf)) and SPhilBERTa is the only purpose-built Greek/Latin/English sentence embedder ([Riemenschneider & Frank](https://aclanthology.org/2023.alp-1.4/)); both are untested against bge-m3 on ligatura's `la→en` fixture.

Keep the half-split tuning ligatura already uses for fusion weights.

## Prioritized experiments

Order is by expected gain per hour, judged from the evidence above. **Every "expected effect" is inference** from other benchmarks; nothing here has been run on ligatura's fixtures. "Primary fixture" is where the effect should be decided; Kempis (49 links) is a sanity check only, since a change of 1–2 links there is noise. Heavy cost means Mac mini.

## Conclusion

The field's 2026 results reframe what "better embeddings" means for ligatura. Swapping in a stronger general embedder or a small off-the-shelf reranker is the move the evidence most consistently says will not help with allusion; general benchmark rank did not predict allusion recall on Latin, and small rerankers trained on question answering reorder by the wrong notion of relevance. What helps is restoring information that pooling and suffix stripping throw away (lemmas, rare word pairs, token-level matches, positional runs) and adapting the encoder to each corpus with its own unlabeled text, all of which fit a local, free tool.

The second shift is about measurement. ligatura's flagship fixture is near its ceiling and is scored against footnotes that likely miss half the links, while its cross-lingual fixtures show the real gap at 7–29% R@10. Adding typed allusion gold (Loci Similes first) and reporting recall on the low-overlap subset will show whether a change finds allusions or only re-finds quotations, a distinction that today's numbers cannot make.
